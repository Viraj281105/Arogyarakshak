@chapter 11 | Conclusion

ArogyaRakshak set out to address a problem that sits between medicine and money: the documents a patient is handed after treatment, and the public rules against which those documents could be checked if anyone had the time, language and knowledge to do so. This report has described a platform that reads those documents once, links what it learns through a shared and consent-bounded case context, and applies five sets of public rules — CGHS rates, the IRDAI Master Circular, NPPA ceilings and the PM-JAY and MJPJAY criteria — to produce findings a patient can act on in English, Hindi or Marathi.

The technical contribution lies as much in how the system behaves when it does not know as in what it computes when it does. Kadi resolves entities across scripts while refusing to merge on semantic similarity alone; BillNyay and DawaCheck refuse to compare prices whose basis is unstated; SchemeSetu reports "verification needed" rather than a false negative; BimaNyay cites only provisions it can trace; and every finding carries a deterministic explanation of the rule and data behind it. Uploaded documents are never stored, consent is enforced by the server, and clinical judgement is left to attributable humans.

The engineering is substantial and verified. On 4 November 2026 the system's 1,119 backend tests, 153 web tests and 126 mobile tests passed, its five architecture contracts and eight CI guardrails held, and the running stack processed a synthetic case end to end. Equally, the report has been explicit about what is not yet true: document reading has not been measured on real scans, the language models have not been run with a live key, the mobile client has not been tested on a device, the reference tables are curated subsets, and the evaluation datasets are small and synthetic. These are the targets of the next phase.

What ArogyaRakshak could become is outlined in Chapter 10 — a platform integrated with India's digital health infrastructure, able to read regional scripts and voices, and trusted by patients and the organisations that help them. What it already is, we believe, is a careful demonstration that an AI-assisted system for a high-stakes public domain can be ambitious in scope while remaining honest in every output.
