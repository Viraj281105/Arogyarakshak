## 8.1 How the Project Evolved

The project was approved by the guide in July 2026 on the basis of a technical specification for three modules — BillNyay, SchemeSetu and DawaCheck — linked by Kadi. It inherited a hackathon prototype of the bill auditor, then called BillGuard, whose LLM pipeline had to be re-pointed immediately because the model it used had been deprecated by the inference provider. Over the following four months the scope grew in one direction and shrank in another. It grew when the insurance lifecycle was split into two new modules, DaaviSetu and BimaNyay (ADR-005), when a mobile client was added, and when the clinical-review layer was introduced to stop the system from implying medical authority it did not have. It shrank, in the sense of becoming more honest, whenever a planned technique turned out to be unnecessary, unavailable or unsafe. Table 8.1 records the most important of these changes.

!table Evolution of key design decisions between the approved specification and the current build | 22,30,48
| Topic | Original plan (July 2026) | Current build and reason |
| Similarity search | FAISS + pgvector entity store | Not used. Resolution blocks by case and type, so candidate sets are tiny; the unwired scaffold was deleted (Oct 2026). |
| Transliteration | IndicXlit (AI4Bharat) | Rule-based Devanagari romaniser + Indic phonetic key; IndicXlit's fairseq dependency has no Python 3.11 wheels (#29 open). |
| Cross-lingual matching | IndicSBERT as a scoring signal | Implemented, optional and off by default; may ask but never merge after the Prednisone/Prednisolone finding. |
| Scheme eligibility | RAG over PM-JAY/MJPJAY documents | Deterministic rule engine with reasoning trace; unsourced income limits removed (Sep 2026). |
| Appeal QA | LLM "judge" with scores | Deterministic judge checks against case facts; fixed scores found and removed. |
| Insurance | Inside BillNyay | Split into DaaviSetu (pre-claim) and BimaNyay (post-denial). |
| Data retention | "Temporary" case records | Explicit DELETE (ADR-010) plus a 90-day server-enforced TTL sweep. |
| Access control | None | Per-case 256-bit token, hashed at rest, constant-time check (ADR-009); full accounts deferred (ADR-008). |
| Prices | Compare MRP with ceiling | Establish the price basis first; refuse otherwise (ADR-012). |

## 8.2 Milestones and Work Completed

Figure 8.1 places the work on a timeline drawn from the commit history. Phase 0 (scoping, monorepo scaffolding and the model swap) and Phase 1 (Kadi, four modules, CI) were complete by mid-August. Phases 2 and 3 — BimaNyay, the mobile client, end-to-end wiring of both clients, entity resolution and auto-triggering — closed on 13 September with all assigned issues resolved. Phase 4, covering multilingual output, evaluation and hardening, began on 15 September and is in progress. Within it, the clinical-review and safety-governance layer (late September), a release-candidate pass that validated the stack on PostgreSQL 16 and Docker Compose, the evaluation harnesses, explanation trail and Claim Pack (mid-October), an engineering-foundation pass introducing the service layer, migrations and deployment manifests, and a page-by-page redesign of the web client (October 21) were completed.

## 8.5 Remaining Work

Four groups of work remain. **Validation:** run the mobile client on a device, exercise the LLM path with a real Groq key, and force the "safety unavailable" fallback on PostgreSQL. **Accuracy:** Devanagari OCR, real-document WER/CER, the BMA synonym and substring defects, BimaNyay's reliance on the category string, full CGHS and Schedule-I ingestion, and form-mapping accuracy. **Language:** native-speaker review of every Hindi and Marathi string, including appeal templates. **Integration:** client wiring of BillNyay's appeal and grievance routes, push delivery of reminders, and the deployment decision for the final demonstration (Chapter 10).
