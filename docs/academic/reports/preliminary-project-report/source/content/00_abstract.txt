After a hospital stay, an Indian patient or caregiver is typically left holding four kinds of paperwork: an itemised hospital bill, a cashless pre-authorisation or claim form, sometimes an insurer's letter denying or cutting the claim, and a prescription to be filled at retail prices. Each document can be checked against a public reference — CGHS rate schedules, the IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance Business, NPPA ceiling prices, and the eligibility rules of PM-JAY and MJPJAY — but doing so requires reading English regulatory text, knowing which rule applies and acting before statutory deadlines lapse. With out-of-pocket spending still about 43% of the country's total health expenditure, these checks matter, yet no single tool performs them.

ArogyaRakshak is a patient-facing decision-support platform that reads a person's own documents once and turns them into evidence-backed next steps in English, Hindi and Marathi. A shared intelligence layer, **Kadi**, performs OCR, line-item parsing, entity extraction, identifier redaction and cross-script entity resolution, and makes the resulting case context available — only with the patient's explicit consent — to five domain modules: **BillNyay** (bill audit against CGHS rates and appeal drafting), **DaaviSetu** (pre-authorisation readiness and form filling), **BimaNyay** (denial audit, three-tier IRDAI escalation drafts and deadline tracking), **SchemeSetu** (rule-based PM-JAY/MJPJAY eligibility with a reasoning trace) and **DawaCheck** (per-unit comparison with NPPA ceilings). The design rests on three principles: uploaded documents are never stored; every finding carries a deterministic explanation of the rule and data behind it; and the system refuses to answer — rather than guess — when the evidence is insufficient. Clinical facts are routed to a human reviewer instead of being inferred.

The current build is a FastAPI modular monolith with a Next.js web client and an Expo mobile client, 27 PostgreSQL tables managed by Alembic, and 21 architecture decision records. On 4 November 2026 we re-ran its suites: 1,119 backend tests passed (7 skipped), with 153 web and 126 mobile tests passing and all architecture contracts and CI guardrails holding. Development-set evaluations on synthetic data give procedure-extraction F1 of 0.942, entity-resolution merge precision of 1.000 and denial-mapping accuracy of 0.560; this report presents these figures together with the defects they exposed and the work still required before real-world use.
